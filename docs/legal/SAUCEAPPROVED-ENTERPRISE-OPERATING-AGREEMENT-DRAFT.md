## 1. Formation

The member(s) adopt this Operating Agreement for SauceApproved enterprise LLC (the **Company**), a Connecticut limited liability company formed by filing its formation document with the Connecticut Secretary of the State.

The effective date of this Agreement is **[EFFECTIVE DATE]**.

The Company's principal business address and registered-agent information are maintained in the Company's private records and applicable state filings and are not reproduced in this public repository.

## 2. Purpose

The Company may conduct any lawful business for which a Connecticut limited liability company may be organized, including software, artificial-intelligence systems, ecommerce, digital services, intellectual-property development and licensing, consulting, media, marketing, and related commercial activities.

## 3. Members and ownership

The members, ownership percentages, and initial contributions are stated in **Schedule A**.

No person becomes a member solely by contributing property, labor, software, money, or services. Admission of a new member requires a written amendment or joinder approved as required by this Agreement.

The Company will maintain its authoritative membership ledger in private company records.

## 4. Management and authority

Unless Schedule A expressly provides otherwise, the Company is **member-managed**.

A member may act for the Company only within authority granted by this Agreement, a written company resolution, or other valid authorization. No member or agent may bind the Company to an extraordinary obligation, transfer substantially all Company assets, admit a new member, change ownership percentages, or dispose of material intellectual property without the approval required by this Agreement.

The Company may delegate operational authority to employees, contractors, software agents, or service providers. Delegation does not transfer ownership and remains subject to Company controls, provider permissions, applicable law, and written authorization boundaries.

## 5. Capital contributions

Initial contributions are listed in Schedule A. No member is required to make an additional contribution unless agreed in writing.

A contribution of software, trademarks, copyrights, domains, data, designs, documentation, trade secrets, or other intellectual property transfers ownership only to the extent supported by a separate valid written assignment or other legally sufficient chain-of-title evidence.

## 6. Profits, losses, and distributions

Profits and losses will be allocated among members in proportion to their ownership percentages unless the members adopt a different lawful allocation in writing.

Distributions will be made at times and in amounts determined by the authorized member(s), subject to applicable law, Company obligations, reasonable reserves, taxes, operating needs, and solvency requirements.

No member has a right to demand a distribution of specific Company property.

## 7. Banking, payments, and financial controls

Company funds must be kept separate from personal funds.

Bank accounts, payment processors, merchant accounts, payout destinations, and financial-provider profiles should use the Company's verified legal and tax identity as required by the provider.

Material transfers, new credit obligations, guarantees, loans, or changes to payout destinations require documented Company authorization.

The Company will maintain books and records sufficient to identify revenue, expenses, assets, liabilities, taxes, owner contributions/distributions, and material provider transactions.

## 8. Tax treatment and records

The Company will use the federal, state, and local tax classification applicable to its actual ownership and valid elections.

Nothing in this Agreement creates a tax election by itself.

The Company will maintain its EIN and business-name records consistently across the IRS, banking, payment, accounting, payroll, marketplace, and other systems where that identity is required.

## 9. Intellectual property and software assets

The Company may own software, source code, models, prompts, workflows, databases, brand assets, domains, documentation, trade secrets, inventions, media, designs, and other intellectual property only to the extent the Company has valid ownership rights.

Pre-existing intellectual property remains with its existing owner unless validly assigned.

Third-party and open-source components remain governed by their applicable licenses and are not converted into Company-owned property by this Agreement.

Company records should preserve provenance, license notices, chain-of-title evidence, and the distinction between:
- Company-owned work;
- assigned work;
- licensed third-party work;
- open-source work; and
- confidential or provider-controlled material.

The prepared founder/rights-holder assignment draft at `docs/legal/SAUCEAPPROVED-ENTERPRISE-IP-ASSIGNMENT-DRAFT.md` is a separate instrument and has no transfer effect unless properly completed and executed.

## 10. Confidential information

Members and authorized agents must protect non-public Company information, including credentials, private keys, customer data, security controls, unreleased source code, trade secrets, financial information, and confidential commercial plans.

Public repositories must not contain raw credentials, signing keys, bank information, government identifiers, private addresses, or executed signature pages.

## 11. Contracts and liability

Company obligations should be entered in the Company's legal name when appropriate.

To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, a member is not personally liable solely by reason of being a member for Company debts or obligations.

No person may use the Company to evade personal obligations, misuse customer funds, commingle funds, or represent an unauthorized act as approved by the Company.

## 12. Indemnification

To the fullest extent permitted by Connecticut law, the Company may indemnify a member, manager, officer, employee, or authorized agent for actions taken in good faith within authorized Company duties, except to the extent prohibited by law or arising from fraud, willful misconduct, knowing violation of law, or unauthorized personal conduct.

## 13. Transfers and new members

A member may not transfer governance rights or admit a substitute member except under a written instrument approved as required by this Agreement.

Economic rights may be treated separately from governance rights to the extent permitted by law and the applicable written transfer instrument.

## 14. Dissolution and winding up

The Company may be dissolved upon a legally sufficient event or written approval required by this Agreement.

On winding up, Company assets will be applied first to lawful Company obligations and reserves. Remaining assets will then be distributed in accordance with applicable law, ownership rights, and valid Company records.

## 15. Records and electronic systems

Company records may be maintained electronically.

Authoritative records should identify the effective version, approval or signature status, and provenance. Draft documents must not be represented as executed records.

Electronic signatures and counterparts may be used to the extent valid under applicable law and accepted by the parties.

## 16. Amendments

This Agreement may be amended only in a written record approved by the member(s) whose approval is required under the then-current ownership and governance structure.

No automated process, AI agent, repository commit, database update, or software deployment may amend this Agreement without the required human legal authorization.

## 17. Governing law

This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Connecticut, without regard to conflict-of-law principles that would require application of another jurisdiction's laws.

## 18. Entire agreement; severability

This Agreement and valid written amendments constitute the operating agreement of the Company regarding the matters addressed here.

If a provision is held unenforceable, the remaining provisions remain effective to the extent permitted by law.
